The Hardest Part Wasn't Leaving My Job. It Was Losing My Identity.

If you've read my previous pieces, you might think I'm completely comfortable in my new role.

The truth is — I wasn't. At least not in the beginning.

At first, it actually felt good. No more daily office rush. No more bosses, deadlines, or client pressure. For the first time in years, life slowed down.

But slowly, something else began to creep in. Monotony. Self-doubt. And a strange feeling that I couldn't fully explain.

What exactly was I doing with my life now?

Our family income had become half of what it used to be. Naturally, we cut down on unnecessary expenses and outings. That part was manageable.

The harder adjustment was psychological.

Earlier, I had my own salary every month. Now, my wife transferred a fixed amount into my account for household expenses. And whenever I wanted to buy something for myself, I found myself hesitating.

Should I buy this? Do I really need it? What if I need to ask for extra money later?

I had been earning since I was 20. I had never asked my parents for money. In fact, I had spent most of my adult life contributing financially to my family. So suddenly being in a position where I had to depend financially on someone else felt — unfamiliar.

And then there were people. Friends. Relatives. Neighbours. Even casual acquaintances.

No one said anything directly. But sometimes, in the way they spoke to me, I sensed sympathy. Almost pity. As if they saw me as someone who had somehow "fallen behind."

There were days when I seriously considered going back to work. Even part-time work. Contract work. Anything. Just so I could answer the question: "What do you do?"

But at the same time, my wife was pregnant, and we were preparing to become parents for the first time. And somewhere deep down, I knew this phase of relative calm wouldn't last long.

Still, one fear remained: was I slowly losing the identity I had built for years?

Looking back now, I think that was the real struggle. Not housework. Not money. Identity.

And if you are going through a similar phase in life — talk to someone. Your partner. A friend. Anyone you trust.

Because slowly, over time, you do build a new identity. It just takes longer than you expect.

— Indian House Husband
